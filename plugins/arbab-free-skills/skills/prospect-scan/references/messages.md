# Opening messages

The first message has one job: get a reply. It doesn't sell.

## Shape (2 to 3 short sentences)
1. **Their gap, as something you noticed.** Specific to them, not a generic compliment. "I noticed your site has no way to book online, but you're running Instagram ads."
2. **One line on what you do about it.** "We build WhatsApp booking for salons in Dubai."
3. **A small, free next step and a question.** "Happy to send a free 2-minute check of your site. Who's the right person for this?"

## Never in the first message
- **A price.** Price comes after they reply.
- **A link.** A link in a cold message looks like spam; send it after they reply.
- **A person's name.** Address the business, not an individual.
- **Claims you can't prove:** clients, results, "the best in Dubai".
- **Pressure:** "limited offer", "only today".

## Arabic version
- Natural Gulf Arabic, as a person would write on WhatsApp. Not a word-for-word translation.
- Open with a greeting such as <bdi dir="rtl">«السلام عليكم»</bdi> or <bdi dir="rtl">«صباح الخير»</bdi> (send in the morning).
- Same three parts: their gap, what you do, the free step and the question.

## Spoken opener (for landlines)
Three lines, under 15 seconds: who you are, the one thing you noticed on their site, and "who handles your website/bookings?" Then stop talking and listen.

## Examples (fictional businesses)

- English: Hi, I found Lina Beauty on Google Maps with great reviews but no website. We build simple bilingual websites for salons so new clients can find you and book. Can I send you a free preview idea? Who should I speak to?
